Pregnancy: Basic Guide & Instructions

Pregnancy usually lasts around 40 weeks, calculated from the first day of the mother's last menstrual period (LMP). Follow this complete clinical and daily care guide to support a safe, healthy journey for both mother and baby.

Three Main Stages of Pregnancy

Pregnancy is generally divided into three trimesters. Each stage brings important changes in the baby's development and the mother's body.

Weeks 1–12

First Trimester

During this stage, the baby's basic body structures begin to develop. Hormonal changes may also cause several physical symptoms in the mother.

Baby's Development
  • The early development of the brain and spinal cord begins.
  • The heart and other major organs start to form.
  • The arms, legs, and facial features begin to develop.
Mother's Care
  • Visit a healthcare professional after confirming pregnancy.
  • Take folic acid as recommended by a healthcare professional.
  • Get enough rest and eat nutritious food.
  • Avoid smoking, alcohol, and unsafe medications.
Weeks 13–27

Second Trimester

During this stage, the baby's growth becomes more noticeable. Many mothers begin to feel their baby's movements for the first time.

Baby's Development
  • Bones and muscles continue to develop.
  • Hearing and other senses begin to develop.
  • The baby's movements become more noticeable.
  • Different body organs continue to mature.
Mother's Care
  • Eat a balanced diet and drink enough water.
  • Have an anomaly scan as recommended by your doctor.
  • Pay attention to your baby's movements.
  • Enjoy light walking or suitable physical activity if your healthcare professional says it is safe.
Weeks 28–40

Third Trimester

This is the final stage of pregnancy, when the baby gains weight and prepares for birth. Mothers may experience tiredness, back pain, and difficulty sleeping.

Baby's Development
  • The baby gains weight and body fat.
  • The brain and lungs continue to develop.
  • The baby's body gradually prepares for life outside the womb.
Mother's Care
  • Attend regular antenatal checkups.
  • Monitor your baby's usual movement patterns.
  • Prepare your hospital bag and birth plan.
  • Seek medical attention promptly if you notice any unusual symptoms.

Baby's Development Week by Week

A baby's development changes throughout pregnancy. The following timeline highlights some important stages.

Daily Care During Pregnancy

Taking care of your body during pregnancy is essential. Small, healthy daily habits can support the well-being of both mother and baby.

Healthy Eating Habits

Eat a variety of nutritious foods every day. Include protein, iron, calcium, vitamins, fruits, and vegetables in your daily diet.

Enough Sleep and Rest

Get sufficient sleep and take breaks whenever you feel tired. Proper rest supports both physical and mental well-being.

Light Physical Activity

For uncomplicated pregnancies, walking and gentle exercise may be beneficial when approved by a healthcare professional.

Regular Health Checkups

Attend scheduled Antenatal Care (ANC) appointments. Complete recommended blood tests, blood pressure checks, and ultrasound examinations.

Mental Well-being

Try to reduce stress and maintain a positive environment. Seek support from family members and consult a mental health professional if needed.

What Should You Eat During Pregnancy?

A mother's diet plays an important role in the baby's growth and development. A healthy daily diet should include a variety of essential nutrients.

Nutrient Food Sources Benefits
Protein Eggs, fish, meat, lentils Supports the baby's growth
Iron Meat, lentils, leafy green vegetables Helps produce healthy red blood cells
Calcium Milk, yogurt, cheese Supports the development of bones and teeth
Folic Acid Leafy green vegetables, lentils, fortified foods Supports the baby's neural tube development
Vitamin C Oranges, guavas, lemons Helps the body absorb iron
Fiber Fruits, vegetables, oats, whole grains Helps prevent constipation

Foods and Drinks to Avoid or Limit

  • Avoid alcohol completely during pregnancy.
  • Avoid raw or undercooked eggs, meat, and fish.
  • Avoid unpasteurized milk and dairy products.
  • Avoid fish that contain high levels of mercury.
  • Limit caffeine intake to no more than 200 mg per day.
  • Follow food safety and hygiene practices when preparing meals.

Important Medical Checkups During Pregnancy

Regular Antenatal Care (ANC) is essential for monitoring the health of both mother and baby.

The timing and number of tests may vary depending on the mother's health, medical history, and doctor's recommendations.

Important Health Alert

Warning Signs You Should Never Ignore

Certain symptoms during pregnancy may indicate a serious health problem. Contact a healthcare professional or seek emergency medical care if you experience any of the following:

1 Vaginal bleeding
2 Severe or persistent abdominal pain
3 Severe headache or blurred vision
4 Sudden swelling of the face or hands
5 Reduced or absent baby movements
6 Fluid leaking from the vagina
7 Shortness of breath or chest pain
8 Fever of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher

Emergency Medical Attention

These symptoms may indicate risks to the mother or baby. Do not delay seeking medical care. Reduced baby movements and vaginal bleeding require prompt assessment.

Preparing for Childbirth

As the pregnancy reaches its final stage, it is important to prepare for the baby's arrival in advance.

Hospital Bag Checklist

  • Comfortable clothes for the mother.
  • Clean clothes for the newborn.
  • Baby diapers and soft towels.
  • Personal hygiene and essential care products.
  • Medical reports and prescriptions.
  • Identification documents and hospital papers.
  • Emergency contact numbers.

Birth Planning

  • Choose a suitable hospital or maternity care facility.
  • Arrange reliable transportation for emergencies.
  • Discuss the birth plan with family members.
  • Talk to your doctor about vaginal birth and C-section.
  • Learn about the common signs of labor.
  • Keep important medical documents ready.

Emergency Maternal SOS

Immediate help for danger signs in Bangladesh

Seek immediate care if experiencing:

  • • Excessive vaginal bleeding
  • • Severe headache or blurred vision
  • • Convulsions / fits (খিঁচুনি)
  • • Severe abdominal pain or high fever
  • • Reduced or absent fetal movement
  • • Water breaking before labor
